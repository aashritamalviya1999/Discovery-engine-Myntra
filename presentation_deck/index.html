Problem Definition

High-Intent Shoppers Stall at Bag Because They Can't Trust Cross-Brand Fit

TARGET SEGMENT
High-intent Myntra/AJIO apparel shoppers who shortlisted items with a near-term purchase goal
TRIGGER
Revisiting saved items to judge final checkout readiness for an event, workwear update, or season change
CORE OBSTACLE
Can't reliably predict cross-brand size accuracy and body fit drape before committing to checkout
WORKAROUND TODAY
Reddit sizing threads, Instagram try-on photos, YouTube hauls, comparing past order sizes
USER VALUE
Confidently judge fit without leaving Myntra to search external reviews or try-on content
BUSINESS IMPACT
Stalled Wishlist → Bag progression; fixing it may also reduce fit-related returns downstream
FORMAL PROBLEM STATEMENT
“High-intent apparel shoppers delay moving wishlisted items to Bag because they cannot confidently determine whether their selected size will fit consistently across brands — forcing them to use external reviews, past purchases, and try-on content before committing.”

Solution Rationale

Resolve the Declared Blocker at the Wishlist Stage — No Discounts Required

Opportunity Score Chosen Strategy
Size & Fit Uncertainty 23 / 25 ✓ Primary wedge — MVP core
Comparison Paralysis 22 / 25 Backlog — next iteration
Product Quality / Photo Void 19 / 25 Not addressed in MVP
Price Volatility 17 / 25 Secondary signal only
DESIGN CONSTRAINT
No monetary incentives.
The solution must resolve decision uncertainty itself — not buy the decision with a coupon or discount. Fit confidence is the highest-scoring opportunity that is fully solvable this way.
“A wishlist shouldn't just remember what shoppers like. It should understand what they're waiting for.”
Core Hypothesis: If high-intent users explicitly state what's preventing purchase (Fit, Price, or Both), and Myntra resolves that condition at the wishlist stage, items will progress to Ready to Buy and subsequently to Bag — without any discount.

Solution MVP

Smart Wishlist & FitCheck Turns “Saved” Into “Ready to Buy”

1
Set Fit Anchor
User saves one brand/size that fits well, plus height & fit feedback
2
Declare Blocker
Tag each wishlist item: waiting on Fit, Price, or Both
3
AI Resolves It
FitCheck reasons against anchor; Price Watch tracks real drops
4
Ready to Buy
Item flips state when condition is met — user taps Move to Bag
WHAT MAKES THIS NON-MONETARY
Fit Confidence is the primary, research-backed wedge (#1 Opportunity, 23/25). Price Target Watch tracks organic price changes only — it never creates a discount. Both simply resolve the user's own declared reason for waiting.

Risks & Mitigation

False Fit Confidence Is the Risk That Could Backfire — Here's How We Guard It

Risk Why It Could Happen Mitigation
Low Fit Anchor adoption Manual multi-field setup before any value is delivered Auto-infer anchor from past orders/returns; ask contextually, not upfront
False fit confidence → returns AI-inferred sizing, not real garment measurement data Show confidence bands, not binary calls; feed return data back to recalibrate
Price Watch drifts into discount-like behavior “Notify on price drop” feels like a discount mechanic to users Reactive to organic price changes only; kept secondary to the Fit wedge
Small research sample doesn't generalize 6 interviews + LLM-classified sample are directional, not confirmatory A/B test against a holdout before full rollout; segment by category
Notification fatigue Day-3 nudges on unresolved items feel spammy Cap nudges; suppress on low-confidence items; track opt-out as a guardrail
Highest-priority risk: Accuracy. If FitCheck signals “Ready to Buy” and the item doesn't fit, a stalled wishlist becomes a return — worse than doing nothing.
